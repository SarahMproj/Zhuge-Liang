# Historical Zhuge Liang and Literary Kongming

Zhuge Liang exists in more than one tradition.

The historical statesman and strategist is preserved through official histories, memorials, administrative records, and later scholarship. The literary Kongming of *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* is a heightened cultural figure shaped by centuries of storytelling, drama, folklore, and interpretation.

This project treats both traditions as valuable, but it does not collapse them into one.

## Source labels

Every stratagem should use one of these labels:

- **Historical:** grounded primarily in historical records or writings attributed to Zhuge Liang.
- **Literary:** derived primarily from *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* or later legend.
- **Composite:** combines historical character themes with literary episodes.
- **Modern interpretation:** a new protocol inspired by the tradition rather than a direct adaptation of one source.

## Why the distinction matters

The project is not trying to prove that every famous story literally happened. It is trying to extract strategic patterns responsibly.

Clear labeling helps us:

- avoid presenting legend as verified history;
- preserve the artistic and cultural value of literary episodes;
- invite better sourcing and scholarship;
- distinguish Zhuge Liang's documented governance and counsel from later supernatural or theatrical portrayals;
- evaluate the protocol on its merits without relying on false authority.

## Contribution requirement

Each proposed stratagem should include:

1. its source classification;
2. a short explanation of the relevant story or documented behavior;
3. citations or a source note when available;
4. a statement identifying which parts are modern interpretation.

The source story inspires the protocol. It does not exempt the protocol from evidence, evaluation, or ethical review.
